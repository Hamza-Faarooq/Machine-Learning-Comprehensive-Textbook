# Chapter 14: Ethics, Fairness, and Responsible AI

## Chapter Overview
Machine learning models are not objective arbiters of truth; they inherit and amplify the biases present in their training data and design[cite: 1]. This chapter covers the mathematical definitions of algorithmic fairness, explainability frameworks, and privacy mechanisms.

## Learning Objectives
* Identify sources of algorithmic bias and feedback loops[cite: 1].
* Compute fairness metrics including Demographic Parity and Equalised Odds[cite: 1].
* Understand the Impossibility Theorem of fairness[cite: 1].
* Explain model predictions using SHAP values based on cooperative game theory[cite: 1].
* Apply Differential Privacy (DP-SGD) to protect individual data points during training[cite: 1].

## Concepts Covered
* Algorithmic Bias and Feedback Loops
* Demographic Parity and Equal Opportunity
* SHAP (Shapley Additive exPlanations) & LIME
* Differential Privacy & The Gaussian Mechanism
* EU AI Act and GDPR Regulations

## Connection to Textbook
The implementations provide programmatic ways to compute the exact statistical fairness metrics defined in Chapter 14. We mathematically demonstrate why satisfying multiple fairness definitions simultaneously is often impossible[cite: 1].
